## Description (copy from here)

You hand over $100 at the checkout. How much of it does the supermarket actually keep? At Kroger, which runs nearly 2,700 U.S. supermarkets, operating profit in its latest fiscal year was about $1.28 for every $100 of sales. So how does a business that thin work at all?

We follow $100 through Kroger's income statement (gross margin vs operating margin vs net margin), build a hypothetical basket to see why not every item has to be profitable, and look at the levers that keep supermarkets in business: sheer volume, fast inventory turnover, supplier credit, store brands, the fight against shrink and, in some models, membership fees. Then we compare a supermarket chain (Kroger), a general-merchandise supercenter (Walmart) and a membership warehouse club (Costco), and explain why their margins can't be compared directly.
